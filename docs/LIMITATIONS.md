# Limitations

Written as findings, not disclaimers. Each one is something measured or
something deliberately not measured, and says which.

## 1. The shipped weights encode a property of this evaluation

`w_pop = 4.0` is the single strongest feature in the ranker (+0.114 on the
public set; setting it to 0 costs −0.061). It is that strong because **target
products sit at the 99.5th percentile of `rating_number`** — a consequence of
sampling sessions from the Amazon 5-core split, not of how people shop.

We know the size of the dependence because **A1 removed it and lost**: refitting
the nine weights on our supplementary corpus dropped `w_pop` from 4.0 to 1.0,
gained **+0.229 MRR** on supplementary validation, and lost **−0.116 MRR** on
the public set, regressing on **every** official slice and **every** robustness
scenario.

> A1 demonstrated strong within-generator generalization but failed
> cross-distribution transfer. The supplementary corpus is
> catalog-metadata-grounded and rewards category, exact-match and IDF signals;
> the public Amazon 5-core sessions carry a much stronger popularity prior. A1
> reduced `w_pop` from 4.0 to 1.0 and increased lexical/category weights,
> improving supplementary MRR while reducing public MRR. Popularity is the
> strongest measured explanation, but **not claimed as the sole factor because
> no post-public ablation was performed.**

**Consequence:** on a corpus without 5-core sampling bias, the shipped weights
are probably wrong, and A1's are probably closer. We ship the ones the official
metric rewards and say so here rather than implying generality we did not test.

## 2. The supplementary corpus is synthetic and ours

`supplementary_dev` was generated by us (`supplementary/generate.py`) from the
same catalog. It is **catalog-metadata-grounded**: constraints are drawn from
product fields, so it rewards exactly the signals that read those fields.

`sup-train` and `sup-val` are the **same distribution** — the split is
scenario-stratified over one generator's output. A gain on `sup-val` therefore
demonstrates **within-generator generalization only**. It is not evidence about
the public set, and it is **not a real-user effect**.

## 3. A2-10 has no public number, and cannot be given one

The Phase 7 pre-registration allows **one public confirmation, for one
finalist**. A1 won finalist selection on supplementary validation and spent it.
A2-10 was eliminated at that step, so its record ends at `sup-val` MRR
**+0.008248** with HR@10 and MTTC exactly invariant.

Whether A2-10 would have passed the public gates is a question the protocol
forbids asking afterwards, because answering it is the multiple-comparisons
error the single-run rule exists to prevent. We do not run it and do not
speculate.

## 4. No cross-session memory, by design and by API

The evaluator supplies an external long-term preference profile per session.
**The evaluation API provides no stable user identity**, so there is no way to
accumulate history across sessions and we did not invent one. The profile is
distilled into bounded evidence and judged against candidate support; the
classification is recorded and given **zero ranking weight** (Phase 6C1 found no
demonstrated target alignment).

We do **not** claim: persisted real user history, cross-session learning,
self-modifying code, or any public gain from profile weighting.

## 5. Portability evidence is path-specific

* The quality, latency and memory set quoted in the submission comes from the
  detached clean-checkout run at commit `6b3cd8b77bcc26f7aec7ef9598d38d3acc90d564`
  on **Python 3.14.6 / Darwin arm64**. Latency and RSS on other hosts are
  unmeasured.
* `score_default` also passed the full suite, reproduced TechnicalScore
  **0.932067** exactly and imported no third-party packages on Ubuntu with
  Python 3.10 and 3.11 in successful [GitHub Actions run
  33294760426](https://github.com/Kairon-2005/techjam2026/actions/runs/33294760426).
* Thread sensitivity **was** measured for the semantic showcase (1/2/4/default
  cores), and every setting cleared its 25 ms cap — that is the one portability
  risk we retired with data.
* **Evaluated on English-language sessions only.** The corpus is one catalog
  category (`Clothing_Shoes_and_Jewelry`), 50,000 products, with English
  simulator messages. Nothing here shows the approach transfers to other
  languages or verticals -- and nothing here shows it fails to, either. It was
  simply not tested.

## 6. Robustness is measured against simulators we wrote

The five robustness scenarios (vague start, uncooperative, genuine override,
category override, contradiction) are perturbations of the official public
sessions, implemented by us. They caught real defects — paraphrase sensitivity
dropped an early agent to 0.671 — but they are **our model of adversity**, not
observed user behaviour.

## 7. Typed hardness enables safe narrowing, not strict enforcement

The implementation distinguishes two cases that must not be collapsed into
"hard filtering":

* An **active positive requirement** can narrow the primary Buying pool only if
  it is near-certain, uncontested, catalog-supported, and backed by a facet with
  enough coverage for missing metadata to be safe. The funnel relaxes eligible
  filters until at least 60 candidates survive and carries a standing bounded
  rescue lane of candidates outside that pool.
* An **explicit negative exclusion** never filters in `score_default`. It is
  omitted from the retrieval query and matching candidates receive a
  confidence-scaled ranking penalty.

The final order therefore still **scores rather than strictly enforces** either
kind of constraint. `hardness="hard"` is typed evidence and a filter-eligibility
signal, not a guarantee. A request such as "nothing above $50" can bias and,
when safely indexed, narrow the primary pool, but rescue and relaxation mean the
returned Top-10 is not guaranteed to obey it. For a real deployment that is a
gap, not a subtlety. The same scored behavior explains why retaining stale
evidence could beat wholesale erasure in the measured override experiment
(0.9233 vs 0.8458).

## 8. What would change our mind

| finding | what it would overturn |
|---|---|
| a public-like corpus without 5-core popularity bias | `w_pop = 4.0`, and probably the whole shipped weight vector |
| a second, independently generated supplementary corpus agreeing with A1 | the "within-generator only" limit on A1's result |
| a second public confirmation budget | A2-10's status as unmeasured rather than rejected |
| observed user sessions | every robustness claim, all of which rest on simulators |
